# Benchmark Comparison: Positioning CGA-Bench

## Where CGA-Bench Sits in the Evaluation Landscape

Medical AI evaluation spans a spectrum from multiple-choice question answering (MedQA, MedMCQA) through dialogue-based assessment (AgentClinic) to fully interactive agent benchmarks (MedAgentBench). At the QA end, benchmarks test factual recall but cannot assess temporal reasoning, action sequencing, or safety-critical decision-making under time pressure. At the agent end, recent benchmarks like MedAgentBench introduce FHIR-based clinical tasks but evaluate only action completion without reference to the clinical practice guidelines (CPGs) that define standard of care. CGA-Bench occupies a distinct position: it evaluates *adherence to time-sensitive clinical treatment protocols* derived directly from published CPG evidence, using structured constraint checking rather than open-ended judgment.

## The Gap CGA-Bench Addresses

No prior benchmark simultaneously enforces four types of clinical constraints: (1) forbidden actions (contraindicated medications and procedures), (2) required actions within deadlines, (3) mandatory action sequencing, and (4) patient-context-dependent conditional rules. Existing benchmarks either use static rubrics (ClinicalBench), rely on LLM-as-judge without ground truth constraints (HealthBench), or evaluate at the diagnosis level without testing treatment execution (AMEGA). Furthermore, none of the surveyed benchmarks provide a *constraint derivation engine* that automatically generates evaluation criteria from CPG graphs, making scenario creation reproducible and auditable rather than manually authored.

## Key Differentiators (Table 1)

As shown in Table~\ref{tab:benchmark_comparison}, CGA-Bench is unique across four dimensions. First, **constraint provenance**: every forbidden/required/sequence constraint traces back to a specific CPG section with evidence level, enabling reviewers to audit the evaluation criteria against primary sources. Second, **temporal constraint enforcement**: actions must occur within clinically defined deadlines (e.g., blood cultures before antibiotics, tPA within 4.5 hours), a dimension absent from QA-based and most agent-based benchmarks. Third, **automated scenario generation**: the PatientGenerator produces clinically valid trap and baseline scenarios from conditional rules, yielding 1,655 potential scenarios across 25 CPG domains versus the 100-300 manually authored scenarios typical of competing benchmarks. Fourth, **multi-evaluator disagreement analysis**: rather than treating a single evaluation pipeline as ground truth, CGA-Bench explicitly quantifies how different evaluation strategies (constraint-based, rubric-based, LLM-judge) disagree on the same agent trace, exposing the evaluation reliability problem that other benchmarks assume away.

## Honest Limitations

CGA-Bench covers 25 clinical domains with 418 scenarios, compared to MedQA's 12,000+ questions or MedAgentBench's 300 FHIR tasks. This reflects a deliberate design choice: each CGA-Bench scenario involves 10-30 expected actions with temporal dependencies, making a single scenario significantly more complex than a multiple-choice question. Domain coverage is limited to emergency and acute care CPGs; outpatient, chronic disease, and surgical domains are not yet represented. The benchmark does not test diagnostic reasoning (covered by MedQA, ClinicalBench) or multi-agent coordination (covered by AgentClinic), focusing instead on the underserved area of treatment protocol adherence evaluation.

\subsection{Measure-theoretic preliminaries}
\label{app:thm-prelim}

Let $(\mathcal{T},\Sigma_{\mathcal{T}})$ be the measurable space of traces
equipped with the $\sigma$-algebra generated by the coordinate maps
$(a_i,t_i,s_i)$, and let $D$ be a probability measure on $\mathcal{T}$.
A projection $\pi:\mathcal{T}\to\mathcal{T}_\pi$ is a measurable surjection onto a
measurable space $(\mathcal{T}_\pi,\Sigma_{\mathcal{T}_\pi})$. Let $\pi_\ast D$
denote the pushforward of $D$. Because all four projections used in
Theorem~\ref{thm:coarsening} collapse continuous coordinates to finite
or countable codomains (patient state is discretised in CGA-Bench,
actions are from a finite vocabulary, timestamps are binned at the
episode time resolution), pushforward measures are purely atomic; we
may freely condition on $\pi(\tau)=y$.

A function $m:\mathcal{T}\to\mathcal{V}$ is \emph{$\pi$-measurable} iff it is
measurable with respect to $\pi^{-1}(\Sigma_{\mathcal{T}_\pi})$, equivalently
iff $m$ factors as $m=f\circ\pi$ for some $\Sigma_{\mathcal{T}_\pi}$-measurable
$f:\mathcal{T}_\pi\to\mathcal{V}$. We write $\mathcal{F}_\pi$ for the class of all such $f$.

\subsection{Proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:separating-pairs}}
\label{app:thm-separating-pairs}

We exhibit, for each of the four projections, a pair of traces
$(\tau_1,\tau_2)$ realisable under a canonical CGA-Bench guideline $G$
such that $\pi(\tau_1)=\pi(\tau_2)$ and $V_G(\tau_1)\neq V_G(\tau_2)$.
All four constructions use actions drawn verbatim from the guideline
text cited, so the traces are admissible episodes in CGA-Bench.

\paragraph{Case (i): $\pi_{\mathrm{term}}$ (terminal-state projection).}
Let $G$ = SSC 2021 Hour-1 Bundle, which mandates sequence
\textsc{blood\_culture} $\prec$ \textsc{broad\_spectrum\_antibiotics}
and timing $t(\textsc{antibiotics})\leq t(\textsc{sepsis\_recognition})+60\min$.
Let $s$ be an adult septic-shock patient and $s_f$ the discharge state
``alive, off vasopressors.'' Consider
\begin{align*}
\tau_1 &= \bigl[(\textsc{bc},5,s),\,(\textsc{abx},20,s),\,(\textsc{disch},240,s_f)\bigr], \\
\tau_2 &= \bigl[(\textsc{abx},5,s),\,(\textsc{bc},20,s),\,(\textsc{disch},240,s_f)\bigr].
\end{align*}
Both traces end at $s_f$, so $\pi_{\mathrm{term}}(\tau_1)=\pi_{\mathrm{term}}(\tau_2)=s_f$.
However $V_G(\tau_1)=\mathbf{0}$ (compliant) while
$V_G(\tau_2)_{\textsc{seq}}=1$ (sequence violation: blood culture drawn
after antibiotics administration, contaminating downstream yield per
SSC 2021 §3.2). \hfill $\square$

\paragraph{Case (ii): $\pi_{\mathrm{aset}}$ (action-multiset projection).}
Let $G$ = SSC 2021 as in Case (i). Let
\begin{align*}
\tau_1 &= \bigl[(\textsc{recog},0,s),\,(\textsc{abx},30,s)\bigr], \\
\tau_2 &= \bigl[(\textsc{recog},0,s),\,(\textsc{abx},90,s)\bigr].
\end{align*}
Both have action multiset $\{\!\{\textsc{recog},\textsc{abx}\}\!\}$,
so $\pi_{\mathrm{aset}}(\tau_1)=\pi_{\mathrm{aset}}(\tau_2)$. But
$V_G(\tau_2)_{\textsc{time}}=1$ (antibiotics at $t=90$ min exceeds the
60-minute Hour-1 window) whereas $V_G(\tau_1)=\mathbf{0}$. \hfill $\square$

\paragraph{Case (iii): $\pi_{\mathrm{nord}}$ (ordered-actions projection).}
Same $G$ and same pair as Case (ii). Ordered action sequences are
$\bigl(\textsc{recog},\textsc{abx}\bigr)$ for both traces, so
$\pi_{\mathrm{nord}}(\tau_1)=\pi_{\mathrm{nord}}(\tau_2)$. Wall-clock
timestamps are erased under $\pi_{\mathrm{nord}}$, so the 60-minute
deadline violation in $\tau_2$ is invisible; again
$V_G(\tau_1)\neq V_G(\tau_2)$. \hfill $\square$

\paragraph{Case (iv): $\pi_{\mathrm{nctx}}$ (context-free projection).}
Let $G$ = AHA 2019 acute ischemic stroke guideline. The constraint
``Anticoagulant therapy is relatively contraindicated in the first 24
hours following acute ischemic stroke'' is activated conditional on
patient state (e.g., INR, presence of hemorrhagic transformation).
Consider the single-action trace
$\tau = \bigl[(\textsc{anticoag},60,\cdot)\bigr]$ evaluated under two
distinct patient contexts:
\begin{itemize}[nosep,leftmargin=1.2em]
\item $s_1$ = acute ischemic stroke, NIHSS 4, no hemorrhagic
  transformation, INR 1.0, $>4.5$~h window: anticoagulant
  is an \emph{allowed} step in secondary prevention planning.
\item $s_2$ = acute ischemic stroke within 24 h, hemorrhagic
  transformation on repeat CT, INR 1.8: anticoagulant is
  \emph{forbidden}.
\end{itemize}
Writing $\tau_j=[(\textsc{anticoag},60,s_j)]$ for $j\in\{1,2\}$,
$\pi_{\mathrm{nctx}}(\tau_1)=\pi_{\mathrm{nctx}}(\tau_2)
=[(\textsc{anticoag},60,\emptyset)]$
(contexts erased). But $V_G(\tau_1)=\mathbf{0}$ and
$V_G(\tau_2)_{\textsc{commit}}=1$ (commission). \hfill $\square$

This completes the proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:separating-pairs}. All four
pairs are instantiated inside CGA-Bench; witness episode IDs are
listed in Table~\ref{tab:thm-witnesses}.

\subsection{Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:coarsening}}
\label{app:thm-main-proof}

Fix a projection $\pi$ and guideline $G$ satisfying the lemma
hypothesis. Let $f\in\mathcal{F}_\pi$ be any $\pi$-measurable evaluator; write
$f=g\circ\pi$ with $g:\mathcal{T}_\pi\to\mathcal{V}$. For each $y\in\mathcal{T}_\pi$,
denote $p_v(y)\coloneqq \mathbb{P}_D\bigl(V_G(\tau)=v\,\big|\,\pi(\tau)=y\bigr)$.

\paragraph{Step 1: per-fibre error.}
Conditional on $\pi(\tau)=y$, the random variable $V_G(\tau)$ has
distribution $(p_v(y))_{v\in\mathcal{V}}$ and $f(\tau)=g(y)$ is a constant.
Hence
\begin{align*}
\mathbb{P}_D\!\left[f(\tau)\neq V_G(\tau)\mid \pi(\tau)=y\right]
&= 1 - p_{g(y)}(y) \\
&\geq 1 - \max_{v\in\mathcal{V}} p_v(y) \\
&= \min_{v\in\mathcal{V}}\bigl(1-p_v(y)\bigr)
   \;\geq\; \min_{v\in\mathcal{V}} p_v(y),
\end{align*}
where the final inequality uses $\sum_v p_v=1$ and is tight when
$|\mathcal{V}|=2$ (our setting up to a binary reduction of $\mathcal{V}=\{0,1\}^5$).
For the binary case $\mathcal{V}=\{0,1\}$ the Bayes-optimal per-fibre error
is exactly $\min(p_0(y),p_1(y))$, attained by the plug-in classifier
$g^{\star}(y)=\arg\max_v p_v(y)$.

\paragraph{Step 2: marginalisation.}
Integrating over $y\sim\pi_\ast D$,
\begin{align*}
\text{err}_D(f)
  &= \mathbb{E}_{y\sim \pi_\ast D}\!\left[\mathbb{P}_D\!\left[f(\tau)\neq V_G(\tau)\mid \pi(\tau)=y\right]\right] \\
  &\geq \mathbb{E}_{y\sim \pi_\ast D}\!\left[\min_{v} p_v(y)\right]
  \;=\;\varepsilon^{\star}_{\pi}(G,D).
\end{align*}
Taking the infimum over $f\in\mathcal{F}_\pi$ is attained by the plug-in
classifier above, so the inequality is an equality, establishing
\eqref{eq:bayes-error}.

\paragraph{Step 3: positivity.}
Let $(\tau_1,\tau_2)$ be the separating pair from Lemma~\ref{lem:separating-pairs}
with $y_0\coloneqq\pi(\tau_1)=\pi(\tau_2)$ and
$V_G(\tau_1)\neq V_G(\tau_2)$. By hypothesis
$D(\{\tau_1\})>0$ and $D(\{\tau_2\})>0$, and both traces lie in the
atom $\pi^{-1}(y_0)$, so
$\pi_\ast D(\{y_0\})\geq D(\{\tau_1\})+D(\{\tau_2\})>0$. Moreover
$p_{V_G(\tau_1)}(y_0)>0$ and $p_{V_G(\tau_2)}(y_0)>0$, so
$\min_v p_v(y_0)>0$. Contribution to \eqref{eq:bayes-error} is
$\pi_\ast D(\{y_0\})\cdot \min_v p_v(y_0)\geq
\min\!\bigl(D(\{\tau_1\}),D(\{\tau_2\})\bigr)>0$.
Hence $\varepsilon^{\star}_{\pi}(G,D)>0$.

\paragraph{Step 4: entropy bound.}
For the binary reduction $V_G\in\{0,1\}$, per-fibre conditional entropy
is $H(V_G\mid \pi=y)=h_b\!\bigl(\min_v p_v(y)\bigr)$ where $h_b$ is
binary entropy. On $[0,1/2]$ one has $h_b(x)\geq 2x$ (tangent-line
bound at $x=0$), so
$H(V_G\mid \pi=y)\geq 2\min_v p_v(y)$ pointwise. Taking expectation,
$H_D(V_G\mid \pi)\geq 2\varepsilon^{\star}_{\pi}(G,D)$.
For the full multi-bit verdict $\mathcal{V}=\{0,1\}^5$, the same argument
applies to each coordinate independently, giving
$H_D(V_{G,k}\mid \pi)\geq 2\varepsilon^{\star,k}_{\pi}(G,D)$ for each
violation type $k\in\{1,\ldots,5\}$, and chain rule gives
$H_D(V_G\mid \pi)\geq 2\,\max_k \varepsilon^{\star,k}_{\pi}(G,D)$. \hfill $\blacksquare$

\subsection{Empirical plug-in estimator}
\label{app:thm-empirical}

Given a finite sample $\widehat{D}=\{\tau^{(i)}\}_{i=1}^{N}$ with
verdicts $V_G(\tau^{(i)})$, the plug-in estimator of the Bayes error
under projection $\pi$ is
\[
\widehat{\varepsilon}^{\star}_{\pi}
\;=\;
\frac{1}{N}\sum_{y\in\pi(\widehat{D})}
\min_{v\in\mathcal{V}}\;\Bigl|\bigl\{i : V_G(\tau^{(i)})=v,\;\pi(\tau^{(i)})=y\bigr\}\Bigr|.
\]
Computation reduces to three passes over the dataset:
(1)~compute $\pi(\tau^{(i)})$ for each $i$;
(2)~hash-group by $\pi$-value to form fibres;
(3)~in each fibre, count verdicts per class, take the minimum count,
sum over fibres, divide by $N$.
A fibre contributes to the estimator only if it contains at least two
distinct verdict values; singleton fibres and verdict-pure fibres
contribute zero. Define the \emph{mixed-fibre mass}
$\widehat{\mu}^{\mathrm{mix}}_{\pi}\coloneqq
\frac{1}{N}\bigl|\{i : |\{V_G(\tau^{(j)}) : \pi(\tau^{(j)})=\pi(\tau^{(i)})\}|\geq 2\}\bigr|$;
this is the fraction of the dataset that sits in verdict-heterogeneous
$\pi$-fibres, and $\widehat{\varepsilon}^{\star}_{\pi}\leq
\widehat{\mu}^{\mathrm{mix}}_{\pi}/2$ by construction (each mixed
fibre contributes at least 1 minority verdict out of at least 2
members).

\paragraph{Confidence intervals.}
Because $\widehat{\varepsilon}^{\star}_{\pi}$ is a bounded functional of
the empirical distribution, a paired-episode bootstrap with $B=1000$
resamples gives a valid $95\%$ CI:
$\bigl[\widehat{\varepsilon}^{\star,(2.5)}_{\pi},\,\widehat{\varepsilon}^{\star,(97.5)}_{\pi}\bigr]$.
We recommend reporting this CI alongside the point estimate in the
numerical table (Corollary~\ref{cor:empirical-bayes}).

\paragraph{Interpretation.}
$\widehat{\varepsilon}^{\star}_{\pi}$ is the per-episode probability
that the Bayes-optimal $\pi$-measurable evaluator \emph{must}
misclassify the TCC verdict on the CGA-Bench distribution. It is a
population-level quantity: increasing classifier capacity, sample
size, or retrieval depth cannot move it below this floor.

\subsection{Relation to CRES-1D and why there is no contradiction}
\label{app:thm-cres1d}

CRES-1D (App.~\ref{app:thm-cres1d}) trains a classifier on
$\cresOneDCovFreeNFeatures$ coverage-free morphology features and
achieves AUC $\cresOneDCovFreeAUC$ predicting the TCC hard-violation
verdict. The classifier is \emph{not} $\pi$-measurable for any
$\pi\in\{\pi_{\mathrm{term}},\pi_{\mathrm{aset}},\pi_{\mathrm{nord}},\pi_{\mathrm{nctx}}\}$
because its features (inter-action gap, action-type diversity, episode
duration) combine wall-clock timing \emph{and} action identity at the
trace level. The CRES-1D classifier therefore has access to a
strictly finer $\sigma$-algebra than any of the four projections in
Theorem~\ref{thm:coarsening}, and its high AUC does not contradict the
irreducible-error bound.

To see this explicitly: on a $\pi_{\mathrm{nctx}}$-fibre, the coverage-free
classifier returns a single prediction (it cannot see context), so
on the separating pair of Case (iv) it is identical on
$(\tau_1,\tau_2)$ but $V_G(\tau_1)\neq V_G(\tau_2)$: classification
error is at least $\min(D(\tau_1),D(\tau_2))/D(\pi^{-1}(y_0))$ on that
fibre, consistent with~\eqref{eq:bayes-error}. What CRES-1D does
show is that \emph{across} fibres, a non-$\pi$-measurable classifier
can achieve high pooled AUC on CGA-Bench's distribution. This is a
property of the distribution, not of TCC as an evaluator: it says
that TCC verdicts are strongly predicted by trace morphology when
morphology is allowed to vary freely with clinical content, which is
the regime CRES-1D exploits. The present theorem bounds the error of
\emph{any} evaluator that is restricted to a $\pi$-measurable view of
the trace; such evaluators (ASC, CwT, PAF, AO-FA) are precisely the
benchmark-space alternatives to TCC.
